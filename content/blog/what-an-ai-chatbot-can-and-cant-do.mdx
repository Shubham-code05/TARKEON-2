AI chatbots are everywhere now, and so are big promises about them. Some of those promises are true. Many are not. If you run a small business, here is a plain-language guide to what a chatbot can really do for you, and where you still need a human.

## What we mean by an “AI chatbot”

Older chatbots worked like a phone menu: press 1 for timings, press 2 for fees. If a customer typed anything unexpected, the bot got stuck.

Modern AI chatbots understand normal sentences, spelling mistakes and mixed languages. A customer can type “kal sunday ko clinic khula hai kya?” and get a sensible answer. The chatbot reads your business information (prices, timings, services, policies) and answers questions based on it.

That “based on it” part is important. A good business chatbot should only answer from information you have given it.

## What a chatbot does well

### Answers the same questions, day and night

Most small businesses get the same ten questions again and again: timings, location, prices, availability, parking, documents needed. A chatbot answers these instantly at 11 pm or on a Sunday, when you are not available.

### Captures leads you would otherwise miss

When someone messages after hours and gets no reply, they often contact someone else. A chatbot can answer their question and then collect their name, number and requirement, so your team can follow up in the morning.

### Speaks your customers’ language

A well-set-up assistant can reply in English, Hindi or Hinglish, matching how the customer writes.

### Books simple appointments

It can collect a preferred date and time, check them against simple rules and add the booking to a sheet, calendar or [booking system](/services/web-apps).

### Shows you what customers ask

Every conversation tells you something. A monthly summary of common questions shows you what to add to your website, menu or price list.

## What a chatbot can’t (and shouldn’t) do

### It doesn’t know what you haven’t told it

If your new batch timings are not in its information, it can’t know them. Someone has to keep its knowledge up to date, just like your website.

### It can still make mistakes

AI models sometimes give confident answers that are wrong. A responsible setup reduces this a lot: limiting the bot to your own information, telling it to say “let me connect you to the team” when unsure, and testing it with real questions before launch. But no honest provider will promise zero mistakes.

### It shouldn’t give medical, legal or financial advice

A clinic chatbot can share timings, fees and which doctor sees which condition. It should not diagnose symptoms or suggest medicines. The same goes for legal or financial questions.

### It can’t replace relationships

Complaints, negotiations, sensitive situations and high-value sales need a person. A good chatbot recognises these moments and hands over to a human quickly.

### It won’t fix a bad offer

If customers are not buying because of price, quality or service, a chatbot won’t change that. It makes a good business easier to reach. It doesn’t make a weak offer stronger.

## Quick guide: is it a good fit?

| Usually a good fit | Usually not worth it yet |
| --- | --- |
| You get many repeated questions every day | You get only a few enquiries a week |
| Customers message outside working hours | Every sale needs a long, custom discussion |
| Leads get lost in WhatsApp chats | Your prices and services change daily |
| You want enquiries in one organised list | You don’t have your basic information written down yet |

## What it costs to run

There are two kinds of cost:

1. **Setup.** Collecting your information, writing the rules the bot must follow, connecting it to your website or WhatsApp and testing it. This is a one-time cost.
2. **Running costs.** The AI model charges a small amount per conversation. On WhatsApp, Meta also charges for certain kinds of conversations through the WhatsApp Business Platform. For most small businesses, these running costs are modest, but they depend on how many conversations you have, so ask for an estimate before you start.

Be careful of anyone who offers a “lifetime free” WhatsApp bot. Either the costs are hidden somewhere, or it is using unofficial methods that can get your number banned.

## How to get started without wasting money

- **Write down your top 20 questions and answers.** This is useful even if you never build a chatbot.
- **Start with one channel.** Your website or WhatsApp, not both at once.
- **Keep a human in the loop.** Make the hand-off to your team easy and quick.
- **Review conversations weekly at first.** Fix wrong or unclear answers early.
