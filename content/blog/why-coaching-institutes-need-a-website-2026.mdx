Ten years ago, most coaching institutes grew through word of mouth, pamphlets and a board outside the building. Word of mouth still matters. But today, when a parent hears your name from a neighbour, the very next thing they do is search for you on their phone.

What they find in that moment decides whether they call you, or the institute down the road.

## What parents do before they call

Think about how a parent chooses a coaching centre for their child. They usually want answers to the same few questions:

- Which courses and batches do you run, and for which class or exam?
- What are the timings, and is there a batch that fits school hours?
- Who teaches, and how experienced are they?
- What results have past students actually achieved?
- Roughly how much are the fees?
- Where exactly are you, and is it safe and easy to reach?

If they can’t find these answers quickly, many won’t call to ask. They will simply move on to an institute that makes it easy.

## Why a Facebook page or a listing isn’t enough

Social media pages and listing sites are useful, and you should keep them. But they have limits:

- **You don’t control them.** The platform decides the layout, shows ads next to your page and can change the rules at any time.
- **They are hard to search.** Your batch timings from last March are buried under dozens of posts.
- **Listings show your competitors too.** On a directory page, the parent is one tap away from three other institutes.
- **They rarely answer everything.** A listing has a phone number and a few photos, not your full course structure, faculty or fee details.

A website is the one place online that is completely yours. Everything else (your Google listing, Instagram, WhatsApp) should point back to it.

## The pages a coaching website really needs

You don’t need a huge site. A clear, fast website with these pages covers what most parents look for:

1. **Home.** Who you teach, what you are known for and one clear button to enquire or book a demo class.
2. **Courses and batches.** One section per course: class or exam, duration, batch timings, mode (offline or online) and batch size.
3. **Faculty.** Real names, photos, subjects and experience. Parents trust people, not logos.
4. **Results.** Only results you can prove, with the student’s permission: name, year, exam and score. A few real results beat a long list of vague claims.
5. **Fees.** Exact fees or at least a range. Hiding fees completely creates more phone calls, not fewer.
6. **Contact and location.** Map, address, landmarks, phone and WhatsApp, with timings for calls.
7. **FAQs.** Study material, test series, missed classes, refunds, scholarships.

## Make enquiring effortless

The goal of the website is not to look nice. It is to turn a curious parent into a conversation. A few small things make a big difference:

- A **WhatsApp button** on every page, because many parents prefer to message rather than call.
- A **short enquiry form** with only the essentials: name, phone, class and course. Every extra field loses people.
- A **“Book a free demo class”** option during admission season.
- Pages that **load fast on a basic phone**, since many parents will open your site on mobile data.

During admission season, you can also create a separate [landing page](/services/landing-pages) for your ads, so every rupee spent on Google or Instagram leads to a page about that one offer.

## Let technology handle the repeated questions

Admission season brings the same questions hundreds of times: “What is the fee for class 11 PCM?”, “Is there an evening batch?”, “Do you have a weekend batch?”. An [AI assistant](/services/ai-chatbots) on your website or WhatsApp can answer these instantly, in English or Hindi, day and night, and pass the parent’s details to your counsellor.

It doesn’t replace your counsellor. It makes sure nobody waits until the next morning for a simple answer.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Outdated information.** Last year’s batch timings or fees do more damage than no website at all. Keep it current.
- **Fake or unverifiable results.** Parents talk to each other. One exaggerated claim can undo years of reputation.
- **Stock photos of foreign classrooms.** Real photos of your classrooms, teachers and students (with permission) build far more trust.
- **No mobile testing.** If buttons are tiny or text runs off the screen on a phone, most visitors will leave.
- **The website no one can update.** Make sure you can change timings and fees yourself, or have someone on call who can.

## A quick checklist

Before your next admission season, check that a parent can find these in under a minute on their phone:

- Courses, classes and batch timings
- Faculty with photos
- Verified results
- Fees or a fee range
- Location with a map
- A WhatsApp button and a short enquiry form
