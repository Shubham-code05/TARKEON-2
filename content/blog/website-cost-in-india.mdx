Ask five people what a website costs in India and you will get five answers, from “₹2,000” to “₹2 lakh”. They are not all lying. They are quoting for very different things.

This guide breaks a website into its parts, so you can compare quotes properly and know what you are paying for.

## The parts of a website

Every business website, big or small, has the same basic parts:

1. **Domain name**: your address, like yourbusiness.in
2. **Hosting**: the server where the website lives
3. **Design and development**: planning, designing and building the pages
4. **Content**: the text, photos and videos on the pages
5. **Extra features**: booking, payments, chatbots, multiple languages
6. **Maintenance**: updates, backups, security and small changes after launch

Some quotes include all six. Many include only one or two. That is why prices look so different.

## 1. Domain name

A domain is renewed every year. A .in or .com domain for a small business usually costs somewhere between a few hundred and around fifteen hundred rupees a year, depending on the extension and the registrar. First-year offers are often cheaper than renewals, so check the renewal price too.

**Important:** the domain should be registered in your name, with your email, so you always control it. If someone else owns your domain, they effectively own your online address.

## 2. Hosting

Hosting costs depend on how the website is built and how much traffic it gets:

- **Simple, modern websites** can often be hosted on low-cost or entry-level plans, because they are fast and light.
- **WordPress websites** usually need regular shared or managed hosting, typically billed monthly or yearly.
- **Web apps** with logins and databases need a proper server or cloud plan, which costs more and grows with usage.

Ask who owns the hosting account, what it costs after the first year and what happens if traffic grows.

## 3. Design and development

This is the biggest part of the cost, and the part where quotes vary most. Roughly, you will see these options:

| Option | What you get | Things to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIY website builder | You build it yourself with templates | Monthly subscription, your time, limited flexibility |
| Very cheap template sites | A template with your name and photos | Often slow, rarely unique, hard to update or move |
| Freelancer | A custom or semi-custom site | Quality and support vary a lot; check past work |
| Small studio | Custom design, development and support | Compare what is included, not just the price |
| Large agency | Strategy, design, development, a full team | Usually priced for larger companies |

The real question is not “what is the cheapest?” but “what will bring in customers, and keep working for years?”

## 4. Content

Someone has to write the text and provide photos. Many quotes assume you will do this. If you can’t, ask for content writing to be included and priced upfront. Good, clear text often matters more to your customers than fancy design.

## 5. Extra features

These add cost because they add work:

- Online booking or appointment systems
- Payment gateways (the gateway provider also charges a small fee per transaction)
- A Hindi or other language version
- An [AI chatbot](/services/ai-chatbots) for your website or WhatsApp
- Admin dashboards or a [custom web app](/services/web-apps)

## 6. Maintenance

After launch, websites need care: security updates, backups, small text changes and someone to call when something breaks. Some businesses handle this themselves. Others prefer a monthly care plan. Either way, budget for it. A website that nobody maintains slowly breaks.

## Hidden costs to ask about

Before you agree to any quote, ask these questions:

- Is the domain registered in my name? What is the renewal price?
- Who owns the hosting account, and what does it cost after year one?
- Will I get the full code and all logins at the end?
- How many rounds of changes are included?
- What does it cost to add a page later?
- Is GST included or extra?
- What support do I get after launch, and for how long?

## Red flags

- **No written quote or scope.** If it isn’t written down, it will be argued about later.
- **Guaranteed Google rankings.** Nobody can guarantee rankings. Google decides.
- **The developer keeps your domain or hosting in their name**, with no clear hand-over.
- **100% payment upfront** for a new relationship.
- **No examples of real, live work** you can open on your phone.
